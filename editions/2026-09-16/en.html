The Dec. 22 cutoff leaves users a short window to move assets off the platform, and the $422.7 million Arkham figure is an attribution of addresses to the entity rather than a verified accounting of customer balances. Clarity Act Fails Senate Cloture Vote, Dealing Blow to US Crypto Framework

On Tuesday, a procedural motion to advance the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (H.R.3633) failed to reach the Senate's 60-vote cloture threshold, stalling the most comprehensive US digital-asset market-structure bill to date. Crypto equities fell sharply on the news, with Coinbase sliding nearly 9%.

This vote was the industry's best chance to lock market-structure rules into statute rather than leave them to agency discretion, so its failure means US crypto oversight continues to rest on SEC and CFTC rules that a future administration can rewrite. That durability gap keeps pressure on US-facing firms and could push more investment and development toward jurisdictions such as the EU, where MiCA already provides a clearer rulebook.

The bill had already cleared the Senate Banking Committee on a 15-9 bipartisan vote in May 2026, with updated text released by Senator Cynthia Lummis in July 2026, so the setback was procedural (cloture) rather than a rejection on the merits. Industry figures stressed that the SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets comment process and the CFTC's drafting of a market-structure regime under existing Commodity Exchange Act authority were never contingent on Tuesday's vote.

rss · The Block · Sep 16, 05:24 · 11 sources

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The most direct transmission is through listed crypto equity proxies, which sold off immediately — Coinbase fell nearly 9% — while tokens and US-facing trading venues remain exposed to unresolved questions over which assets are securities and which venues may register. The failure also tilts the relative attractiveness of venues and issuers operating under the EU's MiCA framework, affecting where liquidity and listings concentrate over time.

Background

In the US Senate, most legislation needs 60 votes to end debate and proceed — a mechanism called cloture under Senate Rule 22 — so a bill can be blocked even if it would pass with a simple majority. The Clarity Act, formally the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, is the successor to the earlier FIT21 effort and would divide oversight of digital assets between the SEC and the CFTC, defining which tokens count as securities and which as commodities. In the absence of such a statute, US crypto oversight has relied on regulators' interpretation of their existing authority, including the Howey test derived from a 1946 Supreme Court ruling. The European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation is the main alternative rulebook that companies compare against.

Discussion

Industry reaction was pointed but notably measured: Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse said "This one stings," called for a post-mortem and blamed Democratic politics being elevated over good policy, while arguing that Ripple's business momentum and the SEC/CFTC rulemaking path remain intact. Enso co-founder and CEO Connor Howe argued the miss does not send the market back to 2022, but that durability is exactly where the vote matters, since a future chair can rewrite an agency rule without any Senate vote.

Bitcoin Core v32.0rc1 Defaults Four RPCs to PSBTv2, Opening Compatibility Test Window

Bitcoin Core v32.0rc1 was tagged with a verified signature on Sept. 14, and the live release schedule lists Oct. 10 as the aim for the final v32.0 tag, creating a 26-day compatibility testing window. The prerelease defaults four RPCs to PSBTv2, changes the default estimatesmartfee path to combine block-policy and mempool estimators, and rewrites the HTTP server with new header, connection and caching limits.

Although this is prerelease software rather than a consensus-rule change, altering default RPC behavior can silently break wallet parsers, PSBT signers, fee-bumping tools and services that assume PSBTv0 or that rely on previously tolerated arguments and deprecated fields. Node operators and wallet providers therefore have a concentrated window before the planned final tag to detect and fix integration regressions.

The largest performance change is parallel prefetching of transaction outputs during block connection, which defaults to eight workers, supports up to 16 and can be disabled. The rebuilt transaction index uses less than half the disk space, but older releases cannot read the new format, so a downgrade can trigger another multi-hour rebuild; the HTTP rewrite adds an 8,192-byte header limit, stricter malformed-header handling and a default ceiling of 16 RPC connections.

rss · CryptoSlate · Sep 16, 03:50 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The transmission path runs through operational reliability rather than supply or consensus: exchanges, custodial wallets and payment processors that drive Bitcoin Core RPC backends could see temporary deposit, withdrawal or fee-estimation disruptions if they upgrade without testing PSBTv2 defaults and the new HTTP limits. Any such disruption would be venue-level and short-lived, and because v32.0rc1 is a prerelease, most operators are expected to stay on the 31.1 baseline until the final tag.

Background

Bitcoin Core is the reference implementation of the Bitcoin protocol, and its RPC interface lets external programs control the node, including spending from wallets, reading private data and influencing consensus verification. PSBT, defined in BIP 174 and extended to PSBTv2 by BIP 370, is a standardized partially signed transaction format used to pass transactions between wallets, hardware signers and services. Bitcoin Core's fee estimator works by recording statistics about past confirmations and reporting a fee rate likely to confirm a transaction within a target number of blocks.

Symbiosis Bitcoin Bridge Exploit Mints 46B Fake syBTC, Real Losses Near 10 BTC

An attacker exploited two software flaws in Symbiosis' Bitcoin Bridge to turn a 330-satoshi deposit (roughly $0.25) into about 46.1 billion unbacked syBTC tokens through 12 bogus deposits processed across BNB Chain, Ethereum and Rootstock in around four minutes on September 11, 2026. Symbiosis published a post-mortem estimating real losses to liquidity providers and affected users at 9.97 BTC (about $770,000), pledged compensation, and took the Bitcoin Bridge offline while its Bitcoin-side software is rewritten and independently audited.

The exploit shows how two individually modest bugs — a sender-verification error that granted administrator privileges and a fee-accounting sign error — can chain together into unlimited unauthorized minting on a cross-chain bridge, a category that remains the most heavily attacked part of DeFi. It also illustrates a key distinction for risk assessment: the headline 46.1 billion token figure was never backed by real bitcoin, so the actual damage was bounded by the roughly $8 million of value locked in the protocol rather than by the notional size of the mint.

The 46.1 billion syBTC created is more than 2,000 times Bitcoin's 21 million coin cap, yet syBTC supply stood at only 13.91 tokens before the attack, with 11.26 syBTC sitting in liquidity pools paired with WBTC, cbBTC, BTCB and RBTC. Because minting unbacked bridge tokens does not create the real assets needed to redeem them, the attacker could only extract whatever bitcoin-linked liquidity actually existed on the other side — and Symbiosis, with about $8 million in total value locked, had processed roughly $146 million of bridge volume over the prior 30 days, according to DefiLlama. The post-mortem also flagged AI as part of a changing security environment, saying powerful models are making software vulnerabilities cheaper to find, though it did not say whether the attacker used AI.

rss · CoinDesk · Sep 15, 21:43 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

Direct exposure is confined to Symbiosis liquidity providers and users holding BTC-pegged assets in pools paired against syBTC — WBTC, cbBTC, BTCB and RBTC — with the 9.97 BTC loss representing the damaged side of those pools, and the protocol's roughly $8 million TVL bounding how far losses could spread through its own books. When the bridge is offline it removes one route by which BTC-denominated value enters EVM chains and Rootstock, and the incident feeds into sentiment and risk repricing for cross-chain bridge and synthetic-BTC tokens rather than changing spot bitcoin supply or holder balances, since the minted syBTC was never backed by real coins.

Background

A cross-chain bridge is a service that lets users move a token from the blockchain where it natively exists to another network that does not support it, typically by holding the original asset in a pool and issuing a representation of it on the destination chain. Symbiosis' Bitcoin Bridge issues syBTC, a token intended to represent bitcoin held by the system, so that BTC-denominated value can be used across EVM networks and Bitcoin sidechains such as Rootstock; a satoshi is the smallest unit of bitcoin, equal to one hundred-millionth of a BTC. The attack turned on two accounting assumptions: which part of a bitcoin transaction the bridge reads to decide who sent the money, and how the protocol's minimum-fee logic handles a fee value pushed below zero — if a negative fee is subtracted from a deposit amount, the deposit grows instead of shrinking. Symbiosis' Bitcoin Bridge is non-custodial and does not use wrapped tokens on the Bitcoin side, per its own documentation.

Strix AI agent extracts Baseten admin GitHub token in 25 minutes

Security firm Strix.ai published a report saying its AI pen-testing agent obtained a live admin-level GitHub personal access token for Baseten's production repositories in about 25 minutes, after pulling a Baseten image and inspecting its Docker build history. Following the July 13-14 disclosure, Baseten made the Harbor project private and rotated the basetenbot token.

The incident shows how agentic security tooling can compress what was once a multi-day manual recon process into minutes, while also highlighting a long-standing secrets-hygiene failure: credentials baked into container image layers remain recoverable even after the image is published. It also fuels an ongoing debate about how vulnerability disclosures involving named customers should be framed and communicated.

According to the report, the token carried admin and push access to Baseten's main product repository, the GitOps repository driving its clusters, and its Homebrew tap, plus read/write access to other private repositories including customer-specific ones. Strix.ai says it reported the live token on July 13 at 11:10 PM, that the Harbor project was made private the next morning while the token still worked, and that Baseten Security confirmed the token rotation on July 14 at 4:34 PM.

hackernews · bearsyankees · Sep 16, 02:11 · Discussion · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Background

A GitHub personal access token (PAT) is a credential that acts like a password for API and Git operations, and a leaked PAT with broad scopes lets an attacker read and modify code, CI configuration, and deployment tooling. Docker images are built in layers, and secrets that are copied or referenced during a build (for example via ARG, ENV, or copied files) can persist inside the image history, so pulling and inspecting an image can reveal credentials — a pattern previously documented at scale across thousands of Docker Hub images. Baseten is a Series C company offering an AI inference platform that enterprises use to deploy and run proprietary models, which makes its deployment repositories operationally sensitive.

Discussion

The Hacker News thread (196 points, 106 comments) shows mixed sentiment: commenters credit Baseten for a fast, well-documented remediation, but several criticize Strix.ai for effectively using a real vendor as a marketing campaign and for naming the victim when a simpler disclosure would have sufficed. Others question the legality of the probing, compare it to breaking into a neighbor's house, and note that the story nonetheless makes them curious enough to evaluate Strix's tooling.

Binance Lists SpaceX Pre-IPO Perpetual Futures as $2T Valuation Bets Build

Binance Futures has listed a SpaceX pre-IPO perpetual contract (SPCXUSDT), settled in Tether (USDT), letting traders take long or short positions on the company's expected market value before any potential initial public offering. The listing comes as traders build bets around a roughly $2 trillion valuation for the private rocket-and-satellite firm, and follows rival venues such as Bybit, which launched its own SPCXUSDT pre-IPO perpetual with up to 10x leverage.

Pre-IPO exposure to a company like SpaceX was previously confined to venture capital firms, institutional investors and secondary-market buyers meeting accreditation thresholds, so a retail-accessible derivative materially widens who can express a view on private-company valuations. It also marks a further blurring of the boundary between crypto derivatives venues and traditional private-equity markets, with exchanges competing to list synthetic exposure to the most sought-after unlisted names.

The contract is USDT-settled and never expires, meaning its price is anchored to an expected valuation via funding mechanics rather than to actual SpaceX shares, and holding it conveys no equity, voting rights or ownership claim on the company. Because there is no public share price to reference, the index methodology and the rules governing what happens if SpaceX actually IPOs are central to how the contract behaves.

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The transmission runs mainly through crypto derivatives liquidity and sentiment: capital and open interest may shift toward exchange-listed pre-IPO perp markets competing for the same speculative flow, while funding rates and basis on these synthetic contracts become the pricing signal for how traders read SpaceX's implied valuation. Any significant move in the underlying sentiment around a potential SpaceX listing would be expressed first in these listed contracts rather than in SpaceX itself, and venue-level rules on margin, liquidation and IPO settlement determine who ultimately bears that exposure.

Background

Pre-IPO perpetual futures are crypto derivatives that track a private company's expected share price and, like all perpetuals, never expire — instead a periodic funding payment keeps the contract price tethered to its underlying reference. SpaceX, Elon Musk's rocket and satellite-internet company, remains privately held and is one of the most anticipated potential listings in global markets, which is why synthetic products tied to it attract attention. Trading such products gives derivative exposure to an expected valuation, a reference methodology, a venue's margin system and funding mechanics — it does not turn a private company into a public one.
