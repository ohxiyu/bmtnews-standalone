Cosmos Labs Urges Halt on EVM Chains After Shared Bug Drains Three Networks

Cosmos Labs has urged validators of affected networks to halt their chains after a shared vulnerability in its Cosmos EVM module drained funds on three blockchains. The bug was quietly patched last week without a security advisory, drawing criticism from the security community.

This incident exposes the systemic risk of shared infrastructure: a single bug in a widely used module can drain multiple chains simultaneously. It also highlights the dangers of silent patching, which can leave downstream networks vulnerable before they can upgrade.

KiiChain lost nearly 150 million KII tokens (worth about $9 million at the time, later dumped for only $1.6 million BUSD), while TAC lost 3 billion TAC tokens (about $7.5 million) from its staking contract. KiiChain's post-mortem says two of the three underlying defects remain unfixed upstream.

rss · The Defiant · Aug 26, 06:28 · 2 sources

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The exploit directly affected KII and TAC token markets — KiiChain's drained tokens were dumped for $1.6 million in BUSD, briefly crashing the token's price. The event may increase scrutiny on Cosmos-based chains and their reliance on shared modules, potentially affecting sentiment and liquidity for affected ecosystems, though no directional outcome is implied.

Background

Cosmos EVM is an open-source Cosmos SDK module that embeds a full Ethereum Virtual Machine into CometBFT-based chains, allowing developers to deploy Solidity contracts and use standard EVM tooling. A 'silent patch model' means security fixes are released publicly without coordinated disclosure, so chains that don't upgrade immediately may be exposed. Four networks using the module — MANTRA, TAC, KiiChain, and Nesa — were affected, with MANTRA and Nesa halting chains without user fund losses.

Discussion

The original disclosure was branded 'negligent AF' by critics, in line with Cosmos' silent patch policy. KiiChain's post-mortem openly blamed Cosmos Labs' disclosure process, stating that publishing a security fix in the open before privately notifying affected chains 'hands the vulnerability to anyone reading the commit.'

BitMEX Begins Staged Shutdown, Halting New Positions After Aug. 26

BitMEX will enter reduce-only mode at 04:00 UTC on Aug. 26, preventing traders from opening new positions, with exchange trading ending Sept. 23 and withdrawal APIs disabled Sept. 28. The staged closure follows a strategic review by owner HDR Global Trading Limited.

BitMEX is one of the longest-standing crypto derivatives exchanges, so its closure removes a major venue for leveraged trading and forces users to close or migrate positions. The orderly wind-down also highlights consolidation and regulatory pressure across crypto derivatives markets.

After Aug. 26, traders may still reduce existing exposure but BitMEX can force-close positions before the mandatory close on Sept. 23. Remaining balances after Sept. 23 incur an annual fee of 1% or $50, whichever is greater, and API withdrawals end Sept. 28, leaving only manual withdrawals of USDT, USDC, and ETH on Ethereum.

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The closure removes a long-standing derivatives venue and could lead to forced position unwinds and manual stablecoin withdrawals, mainly affecting BTC, ETH, and Ethereum-based USDT/USDC flows. Since the wind-down is orderly rather than insolvency-driven, the market impact is more operational and sentiment-based than systemic.

Background

BitMEX helped popularize perpetual swaps and crypto derivatives but has faced regulatory scrutiny in recent years. Reduce-only mode is a risk-control feature that lets an order close or reduce an existing position without opening a new one, while API withdrawals are programmatic ways for traders and institutions to move funds off an exchange.

Galaxy attributes 1,789 BTC losses to Coldcard hack, 87% unmoved

Galaxy Research has attributed the theft of 1,789.28 Bitcoin from 8,865 addresses to the Coldcard hack, worth $114.7 million at the time of theft. Attackers have not spent 1,561 Bitcoin, or 87.3% of the attributed losses, which remain in attacker-controlled addresses.

This is one of the largest hardware wallet exploits ever documented, and the high percentage of unmoved funds provides a rare opportunity for forensic tracking and potential recovery. The incident may undermine user confidence in hardware wallets and intensify collaboration between exchanges, compliance firms, and law enforcement on blockchain tracing.

The tally partly relies on 221 victim reports covering 790.72 Bitcoin, or 44.2% of the total attributed losses; the median reported loss was 1.04272 Bitcoin, with more than half of reports exceeding 1 Bitcoin. Bitcoin stolen in later attack waves has moved through CoinJoin transactions, peel chains, and other obfuscation methods, while all funds from the first three waves remain unmoved.

rss · Cointelegraph · Aug 25, 16:46 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The theft involves a large Bitcoin supply that could eventually be liquidated if attackers move the funds, potentially affecting exchange liquidity and market sentiment; however, with 87% of the stolen coins still unmoved, immediate market pressure is limited. Galaxy's sharing of attacker addresses with exchanges and law enforcement could lead to funds being frozen, reducing the likely selling volume.

Background

Coldcard is a Bitcoin-only hardware wallet made by Coinkite since 2017; it protects private keys with secure elements and supports air-gapped signing, which is designed to keep keys offline and safe from remote attacks. CoinJoin is a collaborative Bitcoin transaction method that mixes coins from multiple users into one transaction, making it harder to trace funds; a peel chain is a laundry technique that splits a large amount of cryptocurrency into many small transactions to obscure the trail.

US Expands Sanctions to Anyone Operating in Iran's Crypto Sector

On August 24, 2026, the U.S. Treasury's OFAC issued five sectoral determinations under Executive Order 13902, including one covering digital assets, enabling sanctions on any foreign person worldwide operating in or providing services to Iran's digital asset sector. The action, dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, also designated nearly 60 entities, including individuals tied to over $100 million in crypto payments for the IRGC's Qods Force.

This significantly expands the extraterritorial reach of U.S. sanctions, meaning any crypto exchange, payment processor, or service provider worldwide with any Iran-related activity could face designation. It underscores that crypto is increasingly targeted by regulators as a sanctions evasion channel, raising compliance stakes for the entire industry.

The digital asset determination builds on earlier sectoral determinations covering Iran's financial and petroleum sectors, allowing OFAC to designate entities regardless of location. Among the designations, Ivan Obukhov, a UAE-based Ukrainian national, allegedly processed over $100 million in crypto payments facilitating oil sales for the IRGC's Qods Force, and cyber actor Arman Kahzadian was named for seizing a Bitcoin wallet worth over $30,000.

rss · Decrypt · Aug 25, 18:21 · 4 sources

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

This regulatory action increases compliance and legal risk for crypto businesses worldwide, as any person or entity involved in Iran's digital asset sector may now be sanctioned. The transmission to markets could occur through de-risking by exchanges and service providers with potential Iran exposure, potentially reducing liquidity for certain cross-border crypto flows and heightening geopolitical risk sentiment across digital assets.

Background

Sectoral sanctions are targeted restrictions on specific industries within a sanctioned economy, such as energy, finance, or technology, rather than comprehensive country-wide embargoes. Executive Order 13902, signed in January 2020, initially imposed sanctions on additional sectors of Iran's economy, including construction, mining, manufacturing, and textiles; the new digital assets determination extends that authority. A shadow fleet refers to vessels using concealment tactics to smuggle sanctioned goods, often oil, in response to international sanctions. The U.S. has been increasingly targeting Iranian crypto activity, previously sanctioning exchange Nobitex and firms accepting Bitcoin for safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz.

Apple Launches Mac Studio with M5 Max and M5 Ultra

Apple has introduced the new Mac Studio powered by the M5 Max and M5 Ultra chips, with up to 256GB of unified memory and a strong emphasis on on-device AI performance. The M5 Ultra pairs two M5 Max dies via UltraFusion, delivering over 4.4TB/s of inter-die bandwidth.

This release marks Apple's continued push into local AI, giving developers and professionals a high-bandwidth, large-memory machine to run large language models directly on-device. It signals intensifying competition in the local AI compute market and could shift some AI workloads away from cloud infrastructure.

The M5 Ultra is Apple's first quad-die chip, using UltraFusion to interlink two dual-die M5 Max packages, and the Mac Studio offers up to 1.2TB/s of memory bandwidth. It also includes next-generation PCIe Gen 6-based SSDs with up to double the storage performance and Thunderbolt 5 external connectivity at 120Gb/s.

Background, discussion, and references

Background

Apple Silicon uses a unified memory architecture in which the CPU and GPU share a single pool of memory, eliminating the need to copy data between separate RAM and VRAM. This makes it easier to run large AI models locally because the GPU can access a huge amount of memory directly. The new M5 Ultra extends this approach with a quad-die design, a first for Apple, enabling higher memory capacity and bandwidth. The Mac Studio is Apple's high-end desktop line aimed at professionals.

Discussion

Community reactions are mixed. Some praise Apple for leaning into local AI and hope for optimized versions of frontier open-weight models for the machine, while others criticize the high price of 256GB memory, note the limited future-proofing for models above 1T parameters, and express concerns about thermal performance of PCIe Gen 6 storage.

Qwen Unveils Qwen 3.8-Flash-Next: 125B MoE with 6B Active Parameters

This release could accelerate the local AI trend by making competitive models accessible without data-center GPUs.

Qwen 3.8-Flash-Next uses a mixture-of-experts architecture, where a router selects only a small subset of experts per token, drastically cutting compute cost while retaining the knowledge capacity of 125B parameters. Background, discussion, and references

Background

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models split their weights into many specialized 'experts' and activate only a few per token, giving the speed of a small model with the knowledge of a large one. The key distinction is between total parameters (all weights) and active parameters (those actually used for each token), which determines memory and compute requirements respectively. Qwen is a major open-weight AI lab, and its previous models like Qwen 27B have been popular in the local AI community.

Commenters are eagerly anticipating the model, with several noting it finally justifies owning large-memory consumer devices like Strix Halo or the new Mac Studio.
