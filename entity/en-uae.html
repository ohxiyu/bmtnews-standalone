    Two Binance Employees Detained in UAE Amid Police Inquiries

    Two Binance employees have reportedly been detained in the United Arab Emirates while local police carry out inquiries, according to a report carried by springfieldnewssun.com. The report does not indicate that user funds, customer assets, or platform availability have been affected. The detentions land in the jurisdiction Binance has leaned on most heavily for its post-2023 regulatory reset, where it holds an ADGM global licence and where Abu Dhabi sovereign fund Mubadala took a $2 billion stake in 2025. Any friction between the exchange and UAE authorities therefore touches both its licensing posture and the credibility of the compliance story it has used to rebuild trust with regulators and institutional partners. Police inquiries are described as ongoing and neither the identities of the detained employees nor the specific allegations have been made public, so it remains unclear whether the matter concerns the exchange's corporate activities, individual conduct, or an unrelated issue. No customer funds or trading services have been reported as affected.

    Standard Chartered offers spot BTC, ETH trading in UAE via Dubai FX platform

    Standard Chartered has become the first top global bank to offer spot Bitcoin and Ether trading in the United Arab Emirates, with the service available through its Dubai FX platform. The announcement came in early September 2026. This milestone shows that globally active banks are willing to bring direct crypto exposure into their regulated platforms, which can strengthen institutional confidence in digital assets. Other international banks may follow suit, expanding access for traditional finance clients. The offering covers Bitcoin and Ether, the two largest cryptocurrencies by market value, and runs on Standard Chartered's Dubai FX platform. This makes the bank the first among major global lenders to provide spot crypto trading in the UAE.

    Standard Chartered launches spot Bitcoin and Ether trading in UAE

    Standard Chartered has launched spot Bitcoin and Ether trading for institutional clients in the United Arab Emirates through its Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC)-regulated entity. The bank claims to be the first major global bank and the first Global Systemically Important Bank (G-SIB) to offer such regulated institutional digital asset trading in the region. This is a significant institutional adoption milestone, as a G-SIB offering regulated spot crypto trading through its existing banking platforms signals growing mainstream acceptance of digital assets. It could pressure other major global banks to follow suit and expand regulated crypto access for institutional capital in the Middle East. Eligible institutional clients can access the trading via Standard Chartered's electronic trading channels integrated into its existing platforms. The bank previously launched digital asset custody services in the UAE in September 2024, and in June it entered a banking agreement with crypto exchange CoinMENA to support fiat on- and off-ramps, client money accounts, and virtual account-based transaction management.
