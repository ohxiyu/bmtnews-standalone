    Bitget’s North Korea-linked $352 million hack could drain 76% of its protection fund

    New developments in Bitget's $351.6M wallet breach: the exchange said attack patterns are consistent with North Korean hackers, XRP was the largest single-chain loss, a full loss could consume about 76% of its $464M protection fund, withdrawals remain suspended, Mandiant and SlowMist are investigating, and an onchain analyst linked some stolen XRP to the July AFX hack.

  3. #0309.25

    Bitget Hack Losses Climb to $387M: Here’s What Happened, and Why North Korea Is a Suspect

    Bitget revised the hack loss total upward from $351.6M to $387.5M; confirmed attackers did not steal private keys but breached a backend system in its wallet infrastructure and spoofed transaction data to trigger legitimate-looking transfer approvals; the largest single haul was roughly 103M XRP (~$157M); the outflow has been stopped and the User Protection Fund will cover the full loss.

    State after update: Bitget confirmed a $387.5M loss caused by spoofed transaction data rather than private key theft; the outflow has been stopped, withdrawals remain frozen, and its protection fund will cover the full loss; the North Korea attribution remains a public suspicion without independent confirmation, with Mandiant and SlowMist investigating.
