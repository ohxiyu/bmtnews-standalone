Current outcome

A developer published a working Linux GPU driver for the Apple M4 Mac Mini, reportedly built in about one month with heavy reliance on large language models. The achievement was quickly overshadowed by the disclosure that the author had been banned from the Asahi Linux project for hiding both his extensive LLM use and his background as a former Apple engineer with direct contacts to people involved in Apple Silicon development. If the workflow is reproducible, LLM-assisted reverse engineering could compress the multi-year timelines normally required to write drivers for hardware with no public documentation, potentially bringing GPU acceleration to newer Apple Silicon Macs running Linux. At the same time, the dispute exposes unresolved questions about provenance, disclosure and conflict of interest in AI-assisted kernel contributions. The driver is unofficial and separate from Asahi Linux, which enforces a strict no-AI-contribution policy, making upstreaming into the mainline Linux kernel unlikely through that route. Commenters also flagged that the author's prior Apple employment with contacts in the Apple Silicon teams creates a conflict-of-interest problem, and that the provenance of LLM training data used for such low-level code is itself unverified.
