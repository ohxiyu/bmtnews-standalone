    New ETF Gives Regulated TRON Exposure to Traditional Brokerage Investors

    A newly launched exchange-traded fund now offers investors regulated exposure to TRON (TRX), the native asset of the TRON blockchain, through an ordinary brokerage account rather than a crypto exchange. The product extends the altcoin ETF wave beyond Bitcoin and Ethereum to a top-20 digital asset. It marks a market-structure shift: TRX becomes reachable to investors who cannot or will not open a crypto exchange account, potentially widening the holder base and deepening institutional familiarity with the asset. It also strengthens the precedent that altcoins beyond BTC and ETH can obtain US-listed, regulated wrappers, which matters for the dozens of other pending crypto ETF filings. TRON is a delegated proof-of-stake, smart-contract-capable blockchain whose TRX token can be frozen to obtain Tron Power, giving holders governance weight and energy or bandwidth resources. The asset has also drawn heavy regulatory and law-enforcement scrutiny, with The Wall Street Journal reporting in 2025 that TRON was the channel for more than half of illegal crypto activity in 2024 and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime describing it as a preferred choice for money launderers in Asia.

    Grayscale's Zcash ETF nears $1B as ZEC doubles past $1,500

    Grayscale's Zcash ETF (ZCSH), which began trading on NYSE Arca on Aug. 25, has reached $914.53 million in net assets and $270.95 million in cumulative net inflows, putting it roughly $85 million short of $1 billion. Over the same period ZEC roughly doubled to trade around $1,540 on Sept. 19, while ZEC futures open interest climbed to a record of about $3.5 billion and futures volume crossed $10 billion for the first time since early June. This is a clear example of regulated, brokerage-accessible exposure to a single altcoin expanding at the same time as record leveraged positioning builds in its derivatives market, meaning institutional inflows and speculative leverage are now reinforcing each other in the same asset. It also tests whether a converted Grayscale trust can scale toward the $1 billion mark that has become a benchmark for altcoin ETF viability, which will inform how issuers and regulators approach similar single-asset products. Because ZCSH physically holds ZEC, price appreciation inflates the assets already inside the fund alongside new creations, which is why its $914.53 million asset base is more than three times its $270.95 million cumulative net inflows. Grayscale announced a 3-for-1 forward split on Sept. 18, with shareholders of record at the Sept. 28 close receiving two additional shares per share held and distribution after the Sept. 29 close, cutting per-share net asset value to roughly one-third of its pre-split level.

    Grayscale's Zcash ETF Plans 3-for-1 Split After $233M Inflow Surge

    Grayscale's Zcash ETF (ZCSH) is planning a 3-for-1 share split after a $233 million inflow surge pushed the fund's assets to nearly $890 million. The move follows a sharp rally in ZEC that has also driven mining competition on the Zcash network to record highs. A share split lowers the per-share price of ZCSH, making the ETF easier to buy in smaller amounts and potentially broadening its retail investor base. It also signals that institutional-style, exchange-traded exposure to a privacy-focused asset has grown large enough to warrant routine market-structure housekeeping, a notable step for a category long treated as niche by regulated venues. A 3-for-1 split triples the number of shares outstanding while dividing the price per share by three, leaving the fund's total assets and each holder's overall value unchanged. ZCSH is structured as an exchange-traded product that is not registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, so it does not carry the same regulatory protections as 1940 Act-registered ETFs and mutual funds.
