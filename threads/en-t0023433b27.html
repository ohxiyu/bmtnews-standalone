    Bitcoin Sidechain Liquid Reports $320M Hack, Halts Network

    On September 6, 2026, the Bitcoin sidechain Liquid Network suffered a security exploit that drained roughly 4,000 BTC (~$320 million), prompting network operators to halt all transactions. The attackers have described themselves as ethical security researchers, claiming they are the 'good guys.' Liquid is Bitcoin's main layer-2 sidechain for institutional settlement and digital asset issuance, so a breach of this size undermines confidence in Bitcoin-based custody and sidechain security. This is one of the largest security incidents in the Bitcoin sidechain ecosystem and could renew scrutiny on bridging and pegged-asset risk. Liquid is a federated sidechain run by Blockstream and a group of functionaries, meaning the stolen bitcoin was held under multisig by trusted parties rather than a simple smart contract. The hackers claim to be 'good guys,' implying the exploit may have been framed as a white-hat rescue, and the network suspended block production while the incident was investigated.

    Liquid Network Hacked: $320M in Bitcoin Withdrawn from Sidechain

    The Liquid Network, a Bitcoin sidechain developed by Blockstream, has been hacked, with roughly $320 million (about 4,000 of 4,200 BTC) withdrawn from its federation wallet via SideSwap. The incident was traced to a bug in Blockstream's Elements software, and the network has been paused. This is one of the largest security breaches on a Bitcoin sidechain and raises serious questions about the security model of federated sidechains and the safety of funds bridged to layer-two solutions. It affects Liquid users, exchanges, and institutions using Liquid for tokenized assets and faster trading settlement. The attacker exploited a bug in Elements software to withdraw BTC from the Liquid Federation wallet via the SideSwap protocol. Blockstream has publicly told the hacker that it is 'safe to return the funds,' with the hackers describing themselves as 'good guys,' complicating the question of intent and resolution.

    Hackers Return $270M to Liquid Network After $320M Bitcoin Theft

    Hackers who exploited the Liquid Network returned approximately $270 million in bitcoin after initially stealing about $320 million. The partial return leaves an estimated $50 million in stolen bitcoin still outstanding. Returning the bulk of stolen assets is unusual in crypto heists and may restore some confidence in the Liquid Network, a Bitcoin sidechain that exchanges use for settlement. Even so, the breach highlights security weaknesses in sidechain and custodial infrastructure that many exchange operations depend on. Liquid Network suspended all transactions after the exploit, and the hackers reportedly described themselves as 'the good guys' when returning funds. The precise attack vector and the fate of the remaining stolen bitcoin have not been publicly confirmed.

    Liquid Sidechain Paused After 3,998 BTC Leaves Federation Wallet

    Blockstream's Liquid sidechain disabled its bridge nodes after nearly all the bitcoin reserves backing L-BTC, approximately 3,998 BTC, were withdrawn to a single address. The recipient wrote "we are whitehats" in the transaction. This event is significant because L-BTC is a 1:1 Bitcoin-backed asset, and the safety of its peg depends on the federation wallet's reserves. If the withdrawn Bitcoin is not fully secured and returned, trust in Liquid and similar sidechains could be shaken. The move reduced the federation wallet's Bitcoin reserves to near zero, potentially preventing peg-outs until the coins are returned. The address owner's "we are whitehats" message suggests a claimed rescue, but official confirmation has not been published.
