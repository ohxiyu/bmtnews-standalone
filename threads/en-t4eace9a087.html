    ZRO surges as LayerZero unveils ATLAS exchange infrastructure on Zero blockchain

    On August 25, 2026, LayerZero announced ATLAS, a headless exchange infrastructure built on its Zero blockchain for trading platforms and financial institutions. The system routes 75% of post-venue trading fees into ZRO buybacks and burns and pays trading venues rebates of up to 65%. This marks LayerZero's expansion from an interoperability protocol into institutional capital markets infrastructure. ATLAS enables trading venues and financial institutions to operate their own exchanges without building one from scratch, potentially accelerating institutional adoption of Zero blockchain and creating structural demand for ZRO through its buyback-and-burn mechanism. ATLAS is described as 'headless'—it has no consumer-facing frontend or trading venue of its own; instead it provides matching, clearing, settlement, and risk management as a single integrated system. The platform runs on Zero, a Layer-1 blockchain designed for institutional use, featuring multi-core execution and native interoperability, and backed by firms including Citadel Securities, Ark Invest, DTCC, ICE, and Google Cloud.

    LayerZero loses a dozen partners as ZRO drops 31% in 2025

    LayerZero has lost a dozen partnerships this year, including Nethermind, Kelp DAO, Kraken, and BitGo, as its ZRO token fell by a third. The latest departure, Nethermind, terminated its role as a verifier on Wednesday and migrated to a competitor. This exodus signals declining trust in LayerZero's security model after the Lazarus Group incident, with billions in bridged assets moving off the protocol. As a leading interoperability platform, losing major enterprise and institutional users erodes network effects and could reshape the cross-chain bridge landscape. By early July, more than $7 billion in assets had already migrated off LayerZero, and BitGo's exit pushed the cumulative total toward $15 billion. In July, LayerZero announced it was winding down support for low-activity chains and set an August 3 deadline for teams still using v1 libraries.
