## The Week's Throughline

The decisive development was Washington's coordinated shift from crypto enforcement to formal rulemaking. The SEC proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, which would let issuers raise up to $75 million in 12 months without full registration and offers a conditional safe harbor to end a token's securities status; the CFTC cleared bitcoin perpetual futures for U.S. venues and is exploring rules to bring Hyperliquid onshore; and President Trump hosted crypto executives, pressing for a "fair version" of the Clarity Act. With Coinbase's CEO projecting more than 60 Senate votes for that bill on September 15, the U.S. now has a credible path to a unified federal market structure for digital assets.

## Continuing Threads

- Washington's crypto rulemaking push accelerated: the SEC's proposal includes three fundraising tiers and a Rule 400 safe harbor, comments due October 20; the CFTC's perpetual-futures clearance opens a popular offshore product to regulated U.S. venues; Trump said the CFTC is working to make Hyperliquid fully compliant, sending HYPE up 23%.
- The Clarity Act moved from White House symbolism to a concrete vote count: Trump called it "bullish" in an Oval Office meeting, and Coinbase's CEO publicly predicted more than 60 Senate votes on September 15, following House passage.
- Binance announced delisting of ICX, SCRT, and STORJ for September 3; days later Secret Network executed a 1.079 billion-SCRT mint, cutting existing holders to roughly a quarter of supply, while lead developer SCRT Labs ends support on September 1.
- AI-agent trading went mainstream: Binance launched Agent OS for AI-driven trades with user-set limits, and Coinbase introduced an MCP server that lets AI agents hold wallets and send stablecoin payments.
